1. What is a circle?

A circle is a round shape where every point on the edge is the same distance from the centre.

Think of it: A bicycle wheel, a coin and a round plate are examples of circular objects.
📝 Practical Question: A bicycle wheel is circular and has a radius of 4 cm. What is its diameter?

2. Parts of a circle

Centre

The centre is the exact middle of the circle.

Radius (r)

The radius is the distance from the centre to the edge of the circle.

Diameter (d)

The diameter goes from one side of the circle to the other side through the centre.

Circumference

The circumference is the distance all the way around the circle.

Chord

A chord is a straight line joining two points on the circle. It does not have to pass through the centre.

📝 Practical Question: A round plate has a diameter of 20 cm. What is the distance from the centre to the edge?

🔑 Know Your Circle Letters

Before we use a formula, you must know what each letter means.

r = radius
The distance from the centre to the edge of the circle.

d = diameter
The distance from one side to the other side through the centre.

C = circumference
The distance all the way around the circle.

A = area
The space inside the circle.

π = pi
In this chapter, use π = 3.14.

Remember:
r → radius
d → diameter
C → circumference
A → area

3. Radius and diameter

The diameter is always twice the radius.
DIAMETER (d)
d = 2r
RADIUS (r)
r = d ÷ 2
Example: Radius = 5 cm
Step 1: We need the diameter.
Step 2: d = 2r
Step 3: d = 2 × 5
Answer: 10 cm
📝 Practical Question: A circular garden has a radius of 6 m. What is its diameter?

4. Understanding π (pi)

π is a special number used when working with circles.

π ≈ 3.14
Where did 3.14 come from?
π (pi) is a special number that starts: 3.14159265...

We usually write 3.14 because 3.14159265... rounded to 2 decimal places is 3.14.

So, in this chapter, when we use π, we use 3.14.

Where does 22/7 come in?
π can also be approximated by the fraction 22/7.
You can see why it is close to 3.14 by dividing:
22 ÷ 7 ≈ 3.142857...
When rounded to 2 decimal places, 3.142857... → 3.14.
So 22/7 is a useful way to work with π without a calculator.

Unless the question tells you to use another value, use 3.14 in this chapter.

5. Circumference

Circumference means the distance around the circle.

CIRCUMFERENCE (C)
C = 2πr

Or, if the diameter is given:

CIRCUMFERENCE (C)
C = πd
Example: A circle has radius 4 cm. Find its circumference.
Step 1: Identify the radius: r = 4 cm.
Step 2: Use C = 2πr.
Step 3: C = 2 × 3.14 × 4.
Step 4: C = 25.12.
Answer: 25.12 cm
📝 Practical Question: A round table has a radius of 3 m. How far is it around the table? Use π = 3.14.

6. Area of a circle

Area means the amount of space inside the circle.

AREA (A)
A = πr²

Remember: r² means r × r.

Example: Radius = 5 cm. Find the area.
Step 1: A = πr²
Step 2: A = 3.14 × 5²
Step 3: 5² = 5 × 5 = 25
Step 4: A = 3.14 × 25
Answer: 78.5 cm²
📝 Practical Question: A circular flower bed has a radius of 4 m. What is the area inside it? Use π = 3.14.

7. How do I know which formula to use?

Question says around the circle? → Circumference
Question says inside the circle? → Area
Question gives diameter but needs radius? → Divide by 2
Question gives radius but needs diameter? → Multiply by 2

⭐ Before the Test: Learn These Two Types

These questions may look new, but they use the rules you have already learned. Let’s practise them slowly first.

Type 1: Finding the area

Example: A circle has a radius of 4 cm. Find its area.
Step 1: We are finding the space inside the circle, so we need area.
Step 2: Use the formula: A = πr²
Step 3: Put in the radius: A = 3.14 × 4²
Step 4: 4² means 4 × 4 = 16.
Step 5: A = 3.14 × 16.
Answer: 50.24 cm²

Type 2: Finding the radius when the diameter is given

Example: A circle has a diameter of 14 cm. Find its radius.
Step 1: Remember: the diameter is twice the radius.
Step 2: To find the radius, divide the diameter by 2.
Step 3: Radius = 14 ÷ 2.
Answer: 7 cm
Easy memory rule:
Area → inside → A = πr²
Diameter → radius → divide by 2

Topic 1: First, understand what an equation is

Before we solve anything, we need to understand the words and symbols we are looking at.

1. What is an expression?

An expression is a maths statement that does not have an equals sign.

3x + 5

This is an expression. It tells us what is being calculated, but it does not say that it is equal to something.

2. What is an equation?

An equation is a maths statement with an equals sign (=).

3x + 5 = 20

The equals sign means: the left side has the same value as the right side.

Think of = as a balance.
Both sides must have the same value. We must keep the equation balanced while we solve it.

3. What is the letter?

The letter, such as x, stands for a number we do not know yet. This is called a variable.

x + 5 = 12

We do not know what x is. Our job is to find the number that makes the equation true.

4. What does “solve” mean?

Solve means find the value of the unknown letter.

For example, if we discover that x = 7, we can check it:

7 + 5 = 12 ✓

Because the two sides have the same value, x = 7 is the solution.

5. What are the operations?

We will use four basic operations:

+ means add
− means subtract
× means multiply
÷ means divide

When we solve an equation, we often use the opposite operation to get the letter by itself.

+ and − are opposites.
× and ÷ are opposites.

6. Now let us solve one — slowly

x + 5 = 12
Step 1: We want x by itself.
Step 2: 5 is added to x. The opposite of +5 is −5.
Step 3: Use subtraction: x = 12 − 5
Step 4: Calculate: x = 7
Step 5 — Check: Put 7 back into the original equation: 7 + 5 = 12 ✓

Important: You may hear people say “move the 5 across the equals sign.” This is only a shortcut. The 5 does not physically move. We change the operation to its opposite so the equation stays balanced.

Remember: Understand the equation first. Then identify what is happening to the letter. Then use the opposite operation. Then calculate. Finally, check.

Topic 2: Adding and subtracting

When a number is added to the letter, use subtraction to remove it. When a number is subtracted, use addition.

x + 6 = 14
Step 1: Change +6 to −6.
Step 2: x = 14 − 6
Step 3: x = 8
Check: 8 + 6 = 14 ✓
x − 4 = 9
Step 1: Change −4 to +4.
Step 2: x = 9 + 4
Step 3: x = 13
Check: 13 − 4 = 9 ✓

Topic 3: Multiplication and division

If a number multiplies the letter, divide. If the letter is divided by a number, multiply.

3x = 18
Step 1: 3 multiplies x. The opposite of ×3 is ÷3.
Step 2: x = 18 ÷ 3
Step 3: x = 6
Check: 3 × 6 = 18 ✓

Division — fraction method

x ÷ 4 = 6
or
x/4 = 6
Step 1: Write division as a fraction: x/4 = 6.
Step 2: Multiply both sides by 4.
Step 3: x = 6 × 4 = 24
Check: 24 ÷ 4 = 6 ✓

Practice

Topic 4: Two-step equations

There are two things happening to the letter. Remove them one at a time.

2x + 3 = 11
Step 1: Remove +3. Use −3: 2x = 11 − 3 = 8.
Step 2: Remove ×2. Use ÷2: x = 8 ÷ 2 = 4.
Check: 2(4) + 3 = 11 ✓

Shortcut reminder

+ becomes −   − becomes +   × becomes ÷   ÷ becomes ×

Topic 5: Equations with brackets

First remove the brackets by multiplying the number outside by every term inside.

2(x + 3) = 14
Step 1: Multiply 2 by x: 2x.
Step 2: Multiply 2 by 3: 6.
Step 3: 2x + 6 = 14.
Step 4: Use the shortcut: +6 becomes −6. So 2x = 14 − 6 = 8.
Step 5: ÷2. So x = 8 ÷ 2 = 4.
Check: 2(4 + 3) = 14 ✓

Topic 6: Check your answer

Always put your answer back into the original question.

3x + 2 = 17
If x = 5: 3(5) + 2 = 15 + 2 = 17 ✓

If both sides give the same answer, your solution is correct.

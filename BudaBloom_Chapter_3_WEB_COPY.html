Grade 9 — Like & Unlike Terms

1. What is a Term?

Learn this first — no prior knowledge needed

An algebraic expression is a maths phrase made from numbers, letters and signs.

A term is one part of that expression. The + or − sign separates terms.

For example:

5x + 3

There are 2 terms:

Term 1 = 5x Term 2 = 3

Important: 5x is ONE term. Do not split it into 5 and x. The 5 and x are joined by multiplication: 5 × x.

A term can be a number alone (7), a letter alone (x), or a number and letter together (4x).

If a minus sign is before a term, that term is negative. Example: 3x − 2y + 5 has the terms 3x, −2y and 5.

Worked example — slowly

Look at: 3x − 2y + 5 Step 1: Start at the left. 3x is the first term. Step 2: The − sign tells us the next term is negative: −2y. Step 3: The + sign starts the next term: 5. Answer: 3x, −2y, 5 There are 3 terms.

2. Like Terms

Learn this first

Like terms are terms that have exactly the same letter part.

Look at 4x. The number 4 is the number in front. The letter x is the variable (letter) part.

Look at 7x. The number 7 is different, but the letter part is still x.

So 4x and 7x are like terms.

Examples:

3x and 8x → like 5a and 2a → like 4m² and 9m² → like

But:

3x and 8y → unlike 4m and 4 → unlike

Easy rule: Ignore the number in front for now. Compare the letter part. If the letter part matches exactly, the terms are like.

Worked example — slowly

Compare 4x and 7x. Step 1: Look at the first term: 4x. The letter part is x. Step 2: Look at the second term: 7x. The letter part is x. Step 3: x matches x. Therefore 4x and 7x are LIKE TERMS.

3. Unlike Terms

Learn this first

Unlike terms do not have the same letter part.

Different letters mean unlike:

3x and 4y x ≠ y Therefore: unlike terms.

Different powers also mean unlike. For example, x and x² are not the same letter part.

2x and 5x² x and x² are different Therefore: unlike terms.

Important: Unlike terms cannot be combined into one term. We do not add their front numbers just because both terms contain numbers.

Worked example — slowly

Look at: 3x + 4y Step 1: Look at 3x. Its letter part is x. Step 2: Look at 4y. Its letter part is y. Step 3: Compare x and y. They are different. Therefore 3x and 4y are UNLIKE TERMS. So 3x + 4y stays 3x + 4y.

4. Combining Like Terms

Learn

We can combine ONLY like terms. To combine like terms: 1. Look at the variable part. 2. Add or subtract the numbers in front. 3. Keep the same variable part. The numbers in front are called coefficients, but you do not need to memorise that word yet.

Worked example

3x + 5x Both terms have x, so they are like. 3 + 5 = 8 Keep x: 3x + 5x = 8x

5. Unlike Terms Stay Separate

Learn

If terms are unlike, we do NOT combine them. For example: 4x + 3y x and y are different, so the terms are unlike. We cannot turn this into 7xy or 7. The correct expression stays: 4x + 3y

Worked example

7x + 2 7x has a variable. 2 is a number by itself. They are unlike terms, so they cannot be combined. Answer: 7x + 2

6. Simplifying Expressions

Learn

To simplify an expression: 1. Identify every term. 2. Find the like terms. 3. Combine only the like terms. 4. Leave unlike terms alone. Do not combine terms just because they contain numbers. First check the variable part.

Worked example

Simplify: 2x + 3x + 4 Step 1: Like terms are 2x and 3x. Step 2: 2 + 3 = 5. Step 3: Keep x → 5x. Step 4: 4 is unlike, so it stays. Answer: 5x + 4

Topic 1: What is a term?

Before we combine terms, we need to know what a term is.

3x + 5

A term is one part of an algebraic expression separated by a + or − sign.

Term 1: 3x
Term 2: 5

The 3 is the coefficient. The x is the variable.

Why? The coefficient tells us how many x's we have: 3x means x + x + x.

Extra: An expression can have 3 terms

Sometimes an algebraic expression has three terms. We must be able to identify each term before deciding which ones can be combined.

3x + 5y − 2
Term 1: 3x
Term 2: 5y
Term 3: −2

Why? The plus and minus signs separate the terms. The minus sign belongs to the term after it, so the third term is −2, not just 2.

3x + 5y − 2

Here there are three different types of terms: an x-term, a y-term, and a number. Because they are different, they cannot be combined.

Another 3-term example

4x + 3x + 2
Step 1: Identify the three terms: 4x, 3x and 2.
Step 2: Find the like terms: 4x and 3x.
Step 3: Add their coefficients: 4 + 3 = 7.
Step 4: Keep x: 7x.
Step 5: The 2 has no x, so it stays by itself.
4x + 3x + 2 = 7x + 2

Remember: We combine only terms that are alike. We do not combine 7x and 2.

Topic 2: What are like terms?

Like terms have the same variable with the same power.

3x and 5x

These are like because both have x.

3x and 5y

These are not like because one has x and the other has y.

Important: 4 and 7 are like terms because both are numbers.

Topic 3: How do we combine like terms?

When terms are like, add or subtract their coefficients. Keep the variable.

3x + 5x
Step 1: Identify the like terms: 3x and 5x.
Step 2: Add the coefficients: 3 + 5 = 8.
Step 3: Keep x.
3x + 5x = 8x

Why? 3x means x+x+x and 5x means x+x+x+x+x. Together there are 8 x's.

7y − 3y
Step 1: 7y and −3y are like terms.
Step 2: 7 − 3 = 4.
Step 3: Keep y.
7y − 3y = 4y

Topic 4: Negative terms

The sign belongs to the term. Do not ignore it.

3m − 6m
Step 1: Rewrite it as 3m + (−6m).
Step 2: Combine the coefficients: 3 + (−6) = −3.
Step 3: Keep m.
3m − 6m = −3m

This is why an answer such as 3m would be incorrect: the −6m was not ignored.

Topic 5: Terms with a square

A term can also have a square. The small 2 is called an exponent.

x² = x × x

So x² means x multiplied by itself. The square is part of the term.

3x² + 5x²
Step 1: Identify the like terms: 3x² and 5x².
Step 2: They are like because both have the same variable x with the same power 2.
Step 3: Add the coefficients: 3 + 5 = 8.
Step 4: Keep x².
3x² + 5x² = 8x²

Important: x² and x are not like terms. The variable is the same, but the powers are different.

4x² + 3x − 2x²
Step 1: The like terms are 4x² and −2x².
Step 2: Combine their coefficients: 4 − 2 = 2.
Step 3: Keep x²: 2x².
Step 4: 3x cannot be combined with x², so it stays.
4x² + 3x − 2x² = 2x² + 3x

Remember: To be like terms, the variable and its power must be the same.

3-term practice

Try this one:

5x + 2 + 3x

Which terms are like?

Hint: Look at the variable. 5x and 3x both have x, while 2 is a number.

So first combine 5x and 3x, then keep 2.

5x + 3x + 2 = 8x + 2

When terms cannot be combined

Look at 3x + 2y.

3x and 2y are different terms because they have different variables.

We cannot combine them into one term.

So the expression stays:

3x + 2y

Important: We only combine like terms. Unlike terms stay the same.

What is 1 term?

A term is one number, one letter, or numbers and letters multiplied together.

Examples of 1 term:

  • 7 — one term
  • x — one term
  • 5x — one term
  • −3y — one term
  • 4x² — one term

Example

5x

There is only one part: 5x. So this is a 1-term expression.

Why? There is no + or − separating it from another term.

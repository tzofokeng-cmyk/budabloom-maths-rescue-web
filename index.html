1. What is a Term?

Learn this first \u2014 no prior knowledge needed

An algebraic expression is a maths phrase made from numbers, letters and signs.

A term is one part of that expression. The + or \u2212 sign separates terms.

For example:

5x + 3

There are 2 terms:

Term 1 = 5x\nTerm 2 = 3

Important: 5x is ONE term. Do not split it into 5 and x. The 5 and x are joined by multiplication: 5 \u00d7 x.

A term can be a number alone (7), a letter alone (x), or a number and letter together (4x).

If a minus sign is before a term, that term is negative. Example: 3x \u2212 2y + 5 has the terms 3x, \u22122y and 5.

Worked example \u2014 slowly

Look at: 3x \u2212 2y + 5\n\nStep 1: Start at the left. 3x is the first term.\nStep 2: The \u2212 sign tells us the next term is negative: \u22122y.\nStep 3: The + sign starts the next term: 5.\n\nAnswer: 3x, \u22122y, 5\n\nThere are 3 terms.

Q1. In 5x + 3, what are the terms?
Q2. How many terms are in 2a + 4b \u2212 7?
Q3. What are the terms in 6m \u2212 9?

2. Like Terms

Learn this first

Like terms are terms that have exactly the same letter part.

Look at 4x. The number 4 is the number in front. The letter x is the variable (letter) part.

Look at 7x. The number 7 is different, but the letter part is still x.

So 4x and 7x are like terms.

Examples:

3x and 8x \u2192 like\n5a and 2a \u2192 like\n4m\u00b2 and 9m\u00b2 \u2192 like

But:

3x and 8y \u2192 unlike\n4m and 4 \u2192 unlike

Easy rule: Ignore the number in front for now. Compare the letter part. If the letter part matches exactly, the terms are like.

Worked example \u2014 slowly

Compare 4x and 7x.\n\nStep 1: Look at the first term: 4x. The letter part is x.\nStep 2: Look at the second term: 7x. The letter part is x.\nStep 3: x matches x.\n\nTherefore 4x and 7x are LIKE TERMS.

Q1. Which pair contains like terms?
Q2. Are 6p and 2p like terms?
Q3. Which pair is like?

3. Unlike Terms

Learn this first

Unlike terms do not have the same letter part.

Different letters mean unlike:

3x and 4y\n\nx \u2260 y\n\nTherefore: unlike terms.

Different powers also mean unlike. For example, x and x\u00b2 are not the same letter part.

2x and 5x\u00b2\n\nx and x\u00b2 are different\n\nTherefore: unlike terms.

Important: Unlike terms cannot be combined into one term. We do not add their front numbers just because both terms contain numbers.

Worked example \u2014 slowly

Look at: 3x + 4y\n\nStep 1: Look at 3x. Its letter part is x.\nStep 2: Look at 4y. Its letter part is y.\nStep 3: Compare x and y. They are different.\n\nTherefore 3x and 4y are UNLIKE TERMS.\n\nSo 3x + 4y stays 3x + 4y.

Q1. Which pair contains unlike terms?
Q2. Are 5x and 2x\u00b2 like terms?
Q3. Which pair is unlike?

4. Combining Like Terms

Learn

We can combine ONLY like terms.\n\nTo combine like terms:\n1. Look at the variable part.\n2. Add or subtract the numbers in front.\n3. Keep the same variable part.\n\nThe numbers in front are called coefficients, but you do not need to memorise that word yet.

Worked example

3x + 5x\n\nBoth terms have x, so they are like.\n\n3 + 5 = 8\n\nKeep x:\n\n3x + 5x = 8x

Q1. Which expression can be combined?
Q2. What is 4a + 2a?
Q3. What is 9m \u2212 4m?

5. Unlike Terms Stay Separate

Learn

If terms are unlike, we do NOT combine them.\n\nFor example:\n4x + 3y\n\nx and y are different, so the terms are unlike.\n\nWe cannot turn this into 7xy or 7.\nThe correct expression stays:\n\n4x + 3y

Worked example

7x + 2\n\n7x has a variable.\n2 is a number by itself.\n\nThey are unlike terms, so they cannot be combined.\n\nAnswer: 7x + 2

Q1. Can 4x + 3y be combined?
Q2. What is 5a + 2b?
Q3. What is 7x + 2?

6. Simplifying Expressions

Learn

To simplify an expression:\n\n1. Identify every term.\n2. Find the like terms.\n3. Combine only the like terms.\n4. Leave unlike terms alone.\n\nDo not combine terms just because they contain numbers. First check the variable part.

Worked example

Simplify:\n\n2x + 3x + 4\n\nStep 1: Like terms are 2x and 3x.\nStep 2: 2 + 3 = 5.\nStep 3: Keep x \u2192 5x.\nStep 4: 4 is unlike, so it stays.\n\nAnswer: 5x + 4

Q1. What should you do first when simplifying?
Q2. Simplify 3x + 2x + 4.
Q3. Simplify 6a \u2212 2a + 3b.

Topic 1: What is a term?

Before we combine terms, we need to know what a term is.

3x + 5

A term is one part of an algebraic expression separated by a + or \u2212 sign.

Term 1: 3x
Term 2: 5

The 3 is the coefficient. The x is the variable.

Why? The coefficient tells us how many x's we have: 3x means x + x + x.

\n

Extra: An expression can have 3 terms

Sometimes an algebraic expression has three terms. We must be able to identify each term before deciding which ones can be combined.

3x + 5y \u2212 2
Term 1: 3x
Term 2: 5y
Term 3: \u22122

Why? The plus and minus signs separate the terms. The minus sign belongs to the term after it, so the third term is \u22122, not just 2.

3x + 5y \u2212 2

Here there are three different types of terms: an x-term, a y-term, and a number. Because they are different, they cannot be combined.

Another 3-term example

4x + 3x + 2
Step 1: Identify the three terms: 4x, 3x and 2.
Step 2: Find the like terms: 4x and 3x.
Step 3: Add their coefficients: 4 + 3 = 7.
Step 4: Keep x: 7x.
Step 5: The 2 has no x, so it stays by itself.
4x + 3x + 2 = 7x + 2

Remember: We combine only terms that are alike. We do not combine 7x and 2.

\n

Topic 2: What are like terms?

Like terms have the same variable with the same power.

3x and 5x

These are like because both have x.

3x and 5y

These are not like because one has x and the other has y.

Important: 4 and 7 are like terms because both are numbers.

\n

Topic 3: How do we combine like terms?

When terms are like, add or subtract their coefficients. Keep the variable.

3x + 5x
Step 1: Identify the like terms: 3x and 5x.
Step 2: Add the coefficients: 3 + 5 = 8.
Step 3: Keep x.
3x + 5x = 8x

Why? 3x means x+x+x and 5x means x+x+x+x+x. Together there are 8 x's.

7y \u2212 3y
Step 1: 7y and \u22123y are like terms.
Step 2: 7 \u2212 3 = 4.
Step 3: Keep y.
7y \u2212 3y = 4y
\n

Topic 4: Negative terms

The sign belongs to the term. Do not ignore it.

3m \u2212 6m
Step 1: Rewrite it as 3m + (\u22126m).
Step 2: Combine the coefficients: 3 + (\u22126) = \u22123.
Step 3: Keep m.
3m \u2212 6m = \u22123m

This is why an answer such as 3m would be incorrect: the \u22126m was not ignored.

\n

Topic 5: Terms with a square

A term can also have a square. The small 2 is called an exponent.

x\u00b2 = x \u00d7 x

So x\u00b2 means x multiplied by itself. The square is part of the term.

3x\u00b2 + 5x\u00b2
Step 1: Identify the like terms: 3x\u00b2 and 5x\u00b2.
Step 2: They are like because both have the same variable x with the same power 2.
Step 3: Add the coefficients: 3 + 5 = 8.
Step 4: Keep x\u00b2.
3x\u00b2 + 5x\u00b2 = 8x\u00b2

Important: x\u00b2 and x are not like terms. The variable is the same, but the powers are different.

4x\u00b2 + 3x \u2212 2x\u00b2
Step 1: The like terms are 4x\u00b2 and \u22122x\u00b2.
Step 2: Combine their coefficients: 4 \u2212 2 = 2.
Step 3: Keep x\u00b2: 2x\u00b2.
Step 4: 3x cannot be combined with x\u00b2, so it stays.
4x\u00b2 + 3x \u2212 2x\u00b2 = 2x\u00b2 + 3x

Remember: To be like terms, the variable and its power must be the same.

Topic 1: First, understand what an equation is

\n

Before we solve anything, we need to understand the words and symbols we are looking at.

\n

1. What is an expression?

\n

An expression is a maths statement that does not have an equals sign.

\n
3x + 5
\n

This is an expression. It tells us what is being calculated, but it does not say that it is equal to something.

\n

2. What is an equation?

\n

An equation is a maths statement with an equals sign (=).

\n
3x + 5 = 20
\n

The equals sign means: the left side has the same value as the right side.

\n
Think of = as a balance.
Both sides must have the same value. We must keep the equation balanced while we solve it.
\n

3. What is the letter?

\n

The letter, such as x, stands for a number we do not know yet. This is called a variable.

\n
x + 5 = 12
\n

We do not know what x is. Our job is to find the number that makes the equation true.

\n

4. What does \u201csolve\u201d mean?

\n

Solve means find the value of the unknown letter.

\n

For example, if we discover that x = 7, we can check it:

\n
7 + 5 = 12 \u2713
\n

Because the two sides have the same value, x = 7 is the solution.

\n

5. What are the operations?

\n

We will use four basic operations:

\n
+ means add
\n
\u2212 means subtract
\n
\u00d7 means multiply
\n
\u00f7 means divide
\n

When we solve an equation, we often use the opposite operation to get the letter by itself.

\n
+ and \u2212 are opposites.
\n
\u00d7 and \u00f7 are opposites.
\n

6. Now let us solve one \u2014 slowly

\n

\n

\n

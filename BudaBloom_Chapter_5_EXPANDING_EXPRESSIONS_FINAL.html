Chapter 5 — Binomial Multiply by Binomial

Start from the basics.

When we see two brackets next to each other, we must multiply every term in the first bracket by every term in the second bracket.

Topic 1 — What does expanding mean?

Expanding means remove the brackets by multiplying every term.

(x + 2)(x + 3)

There are 4 multiplications:

x × x = x²
x × 3 = 3x
2 × x = 2x
2 × 3 = 6

Now put them together:

x² + 3x + 2x + 6

Combine the like terms:

3x + 2x = 5x

Answer: x² + 5x + 6

Topic 2 — FOIL with two brackets

FOIL is a memory method. It tells us which four products to make.

F — First

Multiply the first terms.

x × x = x²
↓

O — Outer

Multiply the outside terms.

x × 3 = 3x
↓

I — Inner

Multiply the inside terms.

2 × x = 2x
↓

L — Last

Multiply the last terms.

2 × 3 = 6
Example:
(x + 2)(x + 3)

F: x²
O: +3x
I: +2x
L: +6

x² + 3x + 2x + 6 = x² + 5x + 6

Topic 3 — FMOL

FMOL is another memory method. The real rule is still: multiply every term.

F — First

First × First

x × x = x²
↓

M — Middle

First × Last

x × 3 = 3x

Last × First

2 × x = 2x
↓

O — Outside? No!

In this chapter, use FMOL only as the memory order shown: First, Middle, Outside, Last. Always check the four actual multiplications.

L — Last

2 × 3 = 6

Important: FOIL and FMOL are memory aids. Do not guess the products. Make sure all four pairs are multiplied.

Topic 4 — Signs: + +, + −, − +, − −

When multiplying signs:

+ × + = +
+ × − = −
− × + = −
− × − = +

Example 1 — + +

(x + 2)(x + 3)
x² + 3x + 2x + 6
x² + 5x + 6

Example 2 — + −

(x + 2)(x − 3)
x² − 3x + 2x − 6
−3x + 2x = −x
x² − x − 6

Example 3 — − +

(x − 2)(x + 3)
x² + 3x − 2x − 6
3x − 2x = x
x² + x − 6

Example 4 — − −

(x − 2)(x − 3)
x² − 3x − 2x + 6
−3x − 2x = −5x
x² − 5x + 6

Chapter 8 — Parallel Lines

Learn → See → Understand → Practise → Test

Parallel-line questions can look difficult, but the shapes give you a simple way to remember the rules.

Memory trick:
🟢 Z → Alternate angles → Equal
🟢 F → Corresponding angles → Equal
🟢 U → Co-interior angles → Add to 180°

Important: Z, F and U are memory aids. They help you spot the correct angles; they are not the formal names of the angle rules.

When you see the diagram: look at the corners where the transversal crosses the parallel lines. The yellow arcs show the exact angles being compared.

8.1 What Are Parallel Lines?

Parallel lines are lines that stay the same distance apart and never meet, even if we extend them.

Symbol: Parallel lines are often shown with matching arrow marks. They tell us the two lines are parallel.

8.2 The Transversal Line

A transversal is a line that crosses two lines.

The transversal creates angles where it crosses the parallel lines. We use the Z, F and U shapes to find related angles.

8.3 Z-Shape — Alternate Angles

Z SHAPE

Look at the two parallel lines and the transversal. The lines can form a clear Z-shape.

Z → Alternate → Equal
Alternate angles are on opposite sides of the transversal. When the lines are parallel, alternate angles are equal.
Example: One alternate angle is 70°.
So the other alternate angle is also 70°.

8.4 F-Shape — Corresponding Angles

F SHAPE

The two parallel lines and the transversal can also help you see an F-shape.

F → Corresponding → Equal
Corresponding angles are in the same matching position at the two crossings. When the lines are parallel, corresponding angles are equal.
Example: One corresponding angle is 65°.
So the matching corresponding angle is 65°.

8.5 U-Shape — Co-interior Angles

U SHAPE

Look between the two parallel lines. The two inside angles on the same side of the transversal can form a U-shape.

U → Co-interior → Add to 180°
Co-interior angles are inside the parallel lines and on the same side of the transversal. Their total is 180°.
Example: One angle is 110°.
Find the other angle:
110° + x = 180°
x = 180° − 110°
x = 70°

8.6 Finding Missing Angles

Step 1 — Look for the shape

Ask yourself: Do I see a Z, F or U?

Step 2 — Choose the rule

  • Z: angles are equal.
  • F: angles are equal.
  • U: angles add to 180°.

Step 3 — Calculate

Z example: 75° → x = 75°

F example: 62° → x = 62°

U example: 125° + x = 180° → x = 55°

8.7 Mixed Examples

Example A — Z
The diagram shows a Z-shape. One angle is 82°. Find x.

Answer: x = 82° because Z angles are equal.

Example B — F
The diagram shows an F-shape. One angle is 48°. Find x.

Answer: x = 48° because corresponding angles are equal.

Example C — U
The diagram shows a U-shape. One angle is 115°. Find x.

Answer: x = 65° because 115° + 65° = 180°.
